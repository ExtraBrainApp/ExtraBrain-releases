## What an AI interview copilot should do for job seekers

A useful interview copilot should support the full interview workflow, not just produce quick answers during a stressful call.
For most candidates, that means five practical jobs.

1. Capture the conversation with live transcription.
2. Help identify the current question, constraints, and follow-up opportunities.
3. Turn messy thoughts into clear outlines, STAR structures, tradeoff lists, or technical explanations.
4. Use relevant screen context when a prompt, diagram, coding task, resume, or job description is visible.
5. Preserve transcripts and notes so you can review what happened afterward.

The best tool for you depends on the interview type.
A sales candidate may care about CRM notes and follow-up emails.
A software engineer may care about coding prompts, complexity analysis, debugging context, and system design tradeoffs.
A product manager may care about structured frameworks, metrics, prioritization, and customer language.
A general job seeker may care most about confidence, recall, and post-interview learning.

## How to compare AI interview copilots responsibly

AI interview tools sit in a sensitive area because interviews are evaluations.
A useful copilot should help you prepare, listen, organize, and review.
It should not encourage you to violate rules, hide prohibited assistance, or pretend that generated work is your own unaided performance.

Use this checklist before using any tool in a live interview or assessment.

- Check the employer, school, meeting, and platform rules before using AI assistance.
- Ask whether transcription, screenshots, or note-taking are allowed.
- Prefer practice and review workflows when live assistance is not allowed.
- Avoid tools or prompts that encourage deception, impersonation, or bypassing proctoring.
- Keep sensitive information out of external providers unless you understand and accept the data flow.
- Be ready to explain your own reasoning without relying on generated text.
