## What a teleprompter AI should do in an interview

A useful interview copilot should support your real thinking instead of replacing it.
The goal is not to read robotic answers word for word.
The goal is to keep the conversation organized while you remain responsible for the final answer.

A strong teleprompter AI for interviews should help with:

- Turning a rambling question into a clear answer outline.
- Suggesting STAR structure for behavioral answers.
- Highlighting the likely intent behind a follow-up question.
- Generating clarifying questions for ambiguous coding or system design prompts.
- Summarizing the current conversation so you do not lose the thread.
- Reviewing transcripts and notes after the interview.
- Respecting interview, employer, school, workplace, meeting, and platform rules.

## How to use teleprompter AI responsibly in interviews

AI interview assistance sits in a sensitive category.
Some interviews allow notes, research materials, transcription, or assistive tools.
Some explicitly forbid AI help, screenshots, recordings, or outside assistance.

Use ExtraBrain only where the rules allow AI assistance, transcription, screenshots, or notes.
If the interviewer, employer, school, workplace, or platform policy says not to use AI, do not use it.
If you are unsure, ask before the interview.

Responsible use looks like this:

1. Use AI to prepare before the interview.
2. Use AI to organize your own thoughts when allowed.
3. Use AI to generate outlines, not fake experiences.
4. Use AI to review transcripts and improve afterward.
5. Never use AI to impersonate skills you do not have.
6. Never use AI to bypass assessment, proctoring, or platform rules.

ExtraBrain is most valuable when it supports authentic thinking.
It should help you explain your actual experience, not manufacture a different candidate.

## What to avoid when choosing a teleprompter AI

Avoid tools that promise guaranteed invisibility, undetectable cheating, or assessment bypassing.
Those promises are risky and often misaligned with interview rules.
They also create a dependency that can make you perform worse when the conversation moves beyond scripted answers.

Also avoid tools that do not explain data flow.
If your interview transcript, screen content, audio, or notes may be sent to an external provider, you should know that before the call begins.

Finally, avoid tools that only produce short generic replies.
A useful assistant should help with reasoning, examples, tradeoffs, and follow-up questions.
