## How to Use ExtraBrain Before an Interview

### Build a Role-Specific Practice Base

Start with the job description, your resume, and the strongest stories from your recent work.
Use ExtraBrain as a focused AI second brain for interviews and meetings, not as a broad replacement for every note-taking database.
Keep your session notes, transcripts, screen context, and review material close to the interview workflow.

A simple preparation routine can look like this.

1. Paste or summarize the target role requirements into your prep notes.
2. List the top skills, projects, and examples you want to highlight.
3. Practice likely questions aloud.
4. Use the transcript to find unclear explanations or missing evidence.
5. Rewrite your answers into shorter, more natural versions.

### Practice Behavioral Answers Without Sounding Scripted

AI can make answers too polished if you let it write everything for you.
A better approach is to ask for structure, then keep your own voice.
For example, you can turn a messy story into a STAR outline, then rehearse the answer out loud until it sounds natural.

Strong prompts include:

- "Turn this project story into a concise STAR answer for a senior software engineer interview."
- "Find the missing result or metric in this behavioral answer."
- "Make this answer sound less scripted and more conversational."
- "Give me a 45-second and a 90-second version of this answer."

### Prepare for Coding and System Design Rounds

For coding interviews, focus on explanation quality as much as final code.
Use AI to practice clarifying questions, edge cases, complexity analysis, and tradeoffs.
For system design interviews, use AI to rehearse requirements gathering, API design, data modeling, scaling bottlenecks, and failure modes.

Before using any AI assistance during a real interview, confirm that the rules allow it.

## How to Review After the Interview

The after-interview review is where many candidates improve fastest.
A transcript helps you move beyond vague feelings like "I think it went okay" or "I completely messed up."
You can inspect the actual conversation.

Review the session with questions like these.

- Which answers were too long?
- Where did I miss a constraint?
- Which follow-up questions did I handle well?
- Where did I sound uncertain even though I knew the answer?
- Which stories should I refine before the next round?
- What should I practice again tomorrow?

This turns every interview into training data for the next one.
Even a difficult interview becomes useful when you can extract lessons from it.
