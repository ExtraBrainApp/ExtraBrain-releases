## How I Compared These AI Interview Assistants

For coding interviews, a useful AI assistant needs to do more than generate a polished answer.

I looked at seven criteria:

1. Coding interview usefulness: Can it help with algorithms, system design, edge cases, complexity analysis, or coding-test environments?
2. Real-time context quality: Does it understand live audio, transcript text, screenshots, or the current prompt?
3. Setup friction: Can a candidate realistically get it working before a high-stakes interview?
4. Privacy and data flow: Does interview content stay local, go to a vendor cloud, or depend on external providers?
5. Screen-share behavior: Is the tool a normal visible workspace, a desktop overlay, or explicitly marketed as hidden?
6. Pricing and free access: Can you try it without a huge commitment?
7. Responsible-use posture: Does the product help you think and prepare, or mostly help you pass off generated answers as your own?

That last point matters. Interview policies vary by company, school, and platform. An AI tool can help you structure your thinking, capture notes, rehearse answers, and review missed edge cases. It can also cross a line if you use it to misrepresent skills you do not have. Treat this as a product comparison, not permission to ignore the rules of an interview.

## What AI Interview Assistants Can and Cannot Safely Do

AI interview assistants can be genuinely helpful when they reduce cognitive load. They can transcribe a question, help you remember constraints, turn a vague prompt into clarifying questions, suggest an answer outline, and help you review weak explanations after the call.

They become risky when they encourage you to misrepresent your ability, hide prohibited help, or pass off generated work as your own.

For coding interviews, the responsible line is especially important. A coding round is often designed to evaluate your reasoning, debugging, and communication. If a tool helps you keep the prompt organized, that is one thing. If a tool secretly solves the entire prompt while you pretend the reasoning is yours, many interviewers would see that very differently.

Before using any AI assistant live, check the rules for the company, school, assessment platform, and interview format. If the rules are unclear, use AI for practice and debriefing instead of live answer generation.
