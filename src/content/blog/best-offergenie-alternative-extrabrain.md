## Why candidates look for an OfferGenie alternative

OfferGenie can be useful because it organizes the messy parts of interview preparation.
A candidate can practice common questions, improve resume alignment, and get feedback before a live call.
That is valuable if the problem is preparation discipline.

The issue is different during a real interview.
A live technical or behavioral interview is not a static practice script.
You are listening, watching the prompt, reading the code editor, deciding what to say next, and trying to sound natural.
If the assistant lives in a browser tab or on a second device, the workflow can become distracting.

That is why the best OfferGenie alternative is not just another question bank.
It should support the whole live session: audio, transcript, screen context, reasoning, follow-up questions, post-interview review, and privacy choices.

## Live interview visibility and screen sharing

A common reason candidates compare OfferGenie alternatives is screen sharing.
If your assistant is in a browser tab, you have to think about tab switching, window focus, screen share boundaries, and whether a second device makes you look distracted.
That can create more anxiety than the interview itself.

ExtraBrain takes a desktop-first approach.
It is designed to stay hidden from screen sharing and screen recording on major meeting tools, while still giving you live transcript and screen-context support.
That does not mean every use is allowed, and it does not override interview or workplace rules.
It means the app architecture is better aligned with candidates who need a quieter desktop workflow.

This difference is especially important for interviews where you are already sharing a screen.
You may be showing a code editor, a browser tab, a slide, a whiteboard, or a design document.
A tool that forces constant switching is a poor fit for that environment.

## Coding interview support

A useful coding interview assistant should not just return a final answer.
It should help you reason in a way you can explain.
That means clarifying the problem, naming constraints, discussing brute force and optimized approaches, identifying edge cases, and preparing a clean explanation of the solution.

ExtraBrain is well suited for this because it can work from live transcript and screen-aware context.
If a coding prompt is visible on screen, the assistant can help frame the problem and generate a direction for discussion.
If the interviewer asks why you chose a data structure, it can help outline the tradeoff clearly.
If you finish the interview and want to improve, the session history can become a review asset.

## Privacy and provider control

Privacy is one of the biggest differences between a local-first desktop assistant and a typical web-first interview product.
ExtraBrain gives users clearer control over where sensitive context can go.

A fully local ExtraBrain posture requires local Parakeet transcription plus local Gemma 4 on-device AI where installed and compatible.
In that configuration, transcription and AI prompts can stay local.
If you choose external providers, selected prompts, transcript text, screenshots, audio, or context may be sent to those providers depending on your configuration.

That distinction matters for interview preparation and workplace meetings.
Your resume, interview transcript, company names, customer details, screenshots, and private notes can be sensitive.
A good OfferGenie alternative should make those tradeoffs visible instead of hiding them behind a single cloud workflow.

## Practical setup checklist

Before using ExtraBrain in an interview or meeting, set up the workflow carefully.

1. Confirm that AI assistance, transcription, screenshots, and notes are allowed in your setting.
2. Install ExtraBrain on a compatible Mac.
3. Choose your transcription setup, such as local Parakeet or optional Deepgram.
4. Choose your AI provider, such as local Gemma 4 where installed and compatible, Anthropic, OpenAI, a custom OpenAI-compatible endpoint, Claude Subscription, or Codex Subscription.
5. Test your meeting app, microphone, and screen-sharing setup before the real call.
6. Create or select the profile that matches the interview type, such as coding, system design, behavioral, product, or meeting notes.
7. After the session, review the transcript and notes while the conversation is still fresh.
